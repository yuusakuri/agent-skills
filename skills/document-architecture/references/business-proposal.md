# Business Proposal

Use this structure to support a decision about business improvement, organizational change, or a new initiative. Keep system implementation details secondary unless they are essential to the business decision.

## Inputs

- Verified current-state data and stakeholder evidence
- Business objectives, strategy, policies, and constraints
- Root-cause analysis and candidate interventions
- Resource, cost, benefit, change, and risk assumptions

## Standard structure

| Chapter | Purpose | Required content | Completion check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Executive summary | Enable a quick business decision | Current issue, recommendation, value, required investment, key risk, requested decision | The recommendation and decision are clear on one page |
| 2. Current-state analysis | Establish the baseline | Process, organization, customer experience, performance data, stakeholders, evidence sources | Baselines are dated, sourced, and decision-relevant |
| 3. Problems and root causes | Avoid treating symptoms | Problem statement, affected outcomes, causal analysis, evidence, controllable and external factors | Each proposed action addresses an evidenced cause |
| 4. Goals and KPIs | Define success | Outcome, baseline, target, timeframe, metric definition, data source, owner | Metrics are measurable and resistant to ambiguous interpretation |
| 5. Proposed initiatives | Describe the intervention | Policy, process, organization, service, capability, prioritization, alternatives | Initiatives connect directly to goals and causes |
| 6. Future operations and customer experience | Make change tangible | To-be process, roles, handoffs, controls, customer journey, exception handling | Stakeholders can understand how work changes |
| 7. Execution plan | Show feasible delivery | Workstreams, phases, milestones, dependencies, pilots, decision gates | The plan can start with named owners and prerequisites |
| 8. Organization, roles, and change management | Enable adoption | Governance, decision rights, staffing, communication, training, incentives, resistance management | Adoption responsibilities are funded and owned |
| 9. Resources and cost | Quantify the investment | People, vendor, technology, operating cost, opportunity cost, estimate basis | Cost aligns with the execution plan |
| 10. Quantitative and qualitative benefits | Explain the business case | Revenue, cost, risk, quality, customer, employee benefits; timing and calculation | Benefits distinguish evidence from assumption |
| 11. Risks, assumptions, and mitigations | Expose uncertainty | `RSK-*`, assumption owner, review date, likelihood, impact, mitigation, contingency | Material failure modes have responses |
| 12. Measurement and improvement | Define learning after launch | Instrumentation, review cadence, owner, thresholds, corrective actions, stop or scale criteria | The initiative can be governed after approval |
| 13. Alternatives | Demonstrate choice quality | Status quo, reduced scope, alternative intervention, criteria, trade-offs | Recommendation is not the only option presented |
| 14. Decision and next actions | Secure commitment | Requested approval, owner, first action, due date, unresolved condition | The proposal has an executable next step |

## Writing rules

- Explain the business mechanism by which each initiative produces the expected outcome.
- Avoid unsupported precision in forecasts. Show assumptions and sensitivity.
- Include technology only to the degree required to assess feasibility, cost, risk, or operating change.
- Assign benefit realization to business owners, not only the delivery team.

## Required quality gates

- Root causes, initiatives, KPIs, costs, and benefits form one coherent chain.
- The to-be operation covers roles, controls, exceptions, and adoption.
- Financial and non-financial benefits have owners and measurement methods.
- The proposal presents alternatives and asks for a specific decision.
